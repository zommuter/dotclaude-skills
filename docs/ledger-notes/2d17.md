> **CORRECTION 2026-09-02, and it is a correction to THIS item's own acceptance, found by the
> `id:ff7c`/C3 spec author before anything was built on it.** The clause above is
> self-contradictory in one place and cites the wrong mechanism in another.
>
> 1. It says "an identity-**set** baseline" and then "key the baseline on the **VALUE**".
>    Those are different mechanisms. The VALUE reading is the correct one.
> 2. **"per the ratchet in `id:0d7c`" is WRONG, and provably so.** That ratchet reads a
>    COMMITTED SNAPSHOT (`relay/head-length-baseline.txt`, whose own header says
>    "committed snapshot"), and regeneration is a deliberate separate act. Applied to this
>    item's own founding incident it does not fire:
>
>    `id:718c` went **4,367 -> 222 -> 1,316** chars in one afternoon. With the baseline
>    snapshotted at 4,367, the regrowth to 1,316 is `1316 > 500 and 1316 <= 4367`, which is
>    `length-grandfathered` **WARN** -- reported, never blocking. Only a baseline tightened
>    to the observed 222 makes `1316 > 222` fire as `length-regrowth` ERROR.
>
>    So the mechanism this item cited as its model cannot catch the incident this item was
>    filed for. Citing it would have produced a build that looked correct and was inert --
>    which is `id:0b70`, the vacuous-check class, arriving through the acceptance criterion
>    rather than through the checker.
>
> **DECIDED 2026-09-02 (owner): MANDATORY REGEN plus a STALENESS DETECTOR.** Self-tightening
> was the alternative and was NOT chosen. So the baseline stays a committed snapshot that
> only a deliberate `--regen-length-baseline` moves, and the thing that makes that safe is a
> separate, READ-ONLY check that reports when the snapshot has gone looser than reality.
>
> Why not self-tightening, recorded so it is not re-argued: it would give a READER a write
> side effect. `todo-conformance.sh` is a reporter, and having it write the committed
> baseline dirties the tree on every run, which the `id:aa93` guard turns into a DEFERRED
> relay dispatch. It would also need a flock (concurrent sessions) and a concept that does
> not exist today -- which invocation is AUTHORITATIVE -- because the checker also runs
> against fixtures, worktrees and hermetic tests, and a fixture run that tightens the real
> baseline poisons it. It does not even remove the regen concept; it inverts when you need
> one, since the only recovery from a wrong tightening is a deliberate regen upward.
>
> Why the detector is what makes regen safe: the failure mode of a snapshot is SILENT
> LOOSENESS, and that is precisely `id:718c`. But "looser than reality" is mechanically
> detectable with NO writes at all -- it is exactly `current < baselined`, a pure read. That
> turns "remember to regenerate" into "the checker tells you to regenerate", which is the
> same contract every other check here has. Precedent for the shape: `mech-currency.sh`
> (`id:0384`) detects that a live proxy's in-memory allowlist is stale versus disk, fails
> closed, names the remedy, and deliberately does NOT auto-restart.
>
> Stated against the decision, so the weakness is on the record: this is TWO mechanisms
> where self-tightening is one, and a detector only helps if someone acts on it, whereas
> self-tightening cannot fail open through inaction. The owner weighed that and chose the
> read-only path anyway.
>
> The detector is `id:2654`, filed separately because it is a gap in the SHIPPED `id:0d7c`
> length ratchet independent of this item. `id:2d17`'s own saturation baseline follows the
> same regen-plus-detect pattern rather than inventing a second one.
>
