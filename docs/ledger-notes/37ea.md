### The hazard, and why a guard was the wrong shape for it

`tools/ledger-shrink.py` (the HEAD-LINE mover) has no cited-body predicate: `grep -c cited`
returns 0, it imports nothing from `ledger-continuations.py`, and its refusals are
cut-point / lane / foreign-id only. The `id:9ce0` + `id:1447` machinery lives entirely on the
BLOCK mover.

That split is backwards relative to the evidence. `9ce0`'s founding note cites two motivating
cases: `roadmap-lint.sh`'s hop-table parser, which "WOULD HAVE" been disarmed (hypothetical),
and `tests/test_contradiction_predicate_5533.sh`, which actually WENT RED. The second was
caused by the head-line mover -- `docs/ledger-notes/931c.md` states its own provenance,
"Detail relocated out of the ledger by `tools/ledger-shrink.py`". **The guard was built where
the hazard was hypothetical and omitted where it fired.**

The investigation confirmed a head-line guard would work: at `f646f072^` the predicate refuses
`id:931c` and names `test_contradiction_predicate_5533.sh:179` as its FIRST ledger-traced site,
on the exact literal, with a positive control (today's union-anchored version of that test
makes the site disappear). It was not rejected for failing.

**It was rejected because every actual incident was already fixed the same way, at the source.**
`6b35`, `c480` and `931c` were each resolved by re-anchoring the CONSUMER to read the ledger
UNION the notes. A guard makes the mover refuse forever; re-anchoring makes the hazard not
exist. And re-anchoring is the only option that also helps repos whose consumers we never
analyse.
