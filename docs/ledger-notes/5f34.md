So `lib-state-claim.sh` is the SIXTH body-reading consumer of relocated bodies, after
`ledger-slice.sh` (`id:2ee1`), the byte gate (`id:f3d2`), `roadmap-lint` rule 3(c)

which is by design: the two were deliberately unified onto one engine so they could never
silently diverge. Fixing the engine fixes both.

**The second finding is the one that generalises, and it is about the GATE, not this
detector.** `tools/shrink-acceptance.py` scores a VIOLATION-polarity record asymmetrically:
a gained finding is FATAL ("the shrink broke the ledger grammar"), a LOST one is an
improvement. That is right for the case it was designed for -- a spurious substring hit
disappearing when prose moves off the line -- and it is exactly wrong for a detector that
went blind, because the two are indistinguishable from the record alone. The gate is
structurally incapable of catching this class, so no amount of re-running it will surface the
next one.

The DISPATCH polarity already has the machinery this needs: `attribute_dispatch_gain()`
requires a gained dispatch id to be EXPLAINED by a suspect marker whose literal text is
present in the item's after-note, and calls it FATAL otherwise. The same attribution run in
the other direction -- a lost VIOLATION must be explained by the triggering text being ABSENT
from the note, not merely moved into it -- would have caught all 40 of these automatically,
and would have caught them before the shrink landed rather than because someone read the
improvements list.

**Fix, in order:** (a) teach the gate to attribute violation LOSSES, since that is the general
guard and it makes every future consumer self-reporting; (b) teach `lib-state-claim.sh` to
follow the per-id detail pointer, the same rule `id:e95b` applied to `roadmap-lint` rule 3(c);
(c) re-run the gate over `3b067459..HEAD` and confirm the 40 come back as findings, which is
the only evidence that (a) and (b) actually work -- a green suite proved nothing here, three
separate times this session.
