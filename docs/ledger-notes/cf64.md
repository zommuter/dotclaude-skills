### The shape

`id:718c` went **4,367 -> 222 -> 1,316** chars in one afternoon, while `shape-prose` was
already firing on that exact line and could not report the regrowth. The regrowth (1,316) is
**smaller than the baselined value** (4,367), so:

- an **id-keyed** baseline forgives it -- the id is in the set, forever. That is the
  `id:cb3e` trap; `lib-state-claim.sh:157` documents that it "silently RE-GRANDFATHERS ...
  There is no expiry".
- a **snapshot** baseline forgives it too -- `1316 <= 4367` is within the recorded floor.
  `head-length-baseline.txt` discloses this in its own words: *"a line listed at 9,000 chars
  stays forgiven at 9,000 chars until this file is regenerated"*.

Only a floor **tightened to the observed shrink** reports it.

### Why this is a separate item and not a defect in `id:2d17`

`2d17` shipped the mechanism the owner ratified on 2026-09-02: *"MANDATORY REGEN plus a
STALENESS DETECTOR ... Self-tightening was the alternative and was NOT chosen."* A snapshot
cannot catch this shape. That is the **boundary** of the ratified design, not a bug in its
implementation, and this item exists to keep the boundary visible instead of leaving it as
an unstated gap inside a green suite.

### Two dead ends, recorded so they are not re-proposed

Both were checked against `2d17`'s own test before the split:

1. **Make `current < baselined` a BLOCKING finding.** It fires at the SHRINK, breaking
   `2d17`'s case (0), *"improving an item never breaks the ratchet"*. Blocking-on-stale and
   clean-on-shrink are mutually exclusive.
2. **Make it a WARN.** Then it does not fail `--strict`, and this item's case (1) still
   fails.

There is no configuration of the snapshot design that satisfies both.

### The route that works, and why it is gated

`id:2654`'s **read-only staleness detector** reports that the floor has gone looser than
reality (`current < baselined` is a pure read, no writes, no flock, no
which-invocation-is-authoritative problem). A human then regenerates, and the regrowth is
caught on the next run.

This is a **weaker guarantee than self-tightening** -- it fails open through inaction,
whereas self-tightening cannot. The owner weighed exactly that trade on 2026-09-02 and chose
the read-only path anyway, on the grounds that a reader must not have a write side effect
(tree dirt feeds the `id:aa93` deferral, and this checker runs constantly against fixtures,
worktrees and hermetic tests where a tightening run would poison the real baseline).

Hence `gated-on:2654`. Without the detector there is no signal that a regen is owed, and
"remember to regenerate" is not a mechanism.

### What the green means, and what it does not

The `id:718c` shape is now caught by a **loop that requires a human act**: the detector
reports, a person regenerates, the regrowth then fires as an ERROR. It is **not** caught
automatically. The loop fails open through inaction -- the accepted weakness of
regen-plus-detect over self-tightening, which the owner weighed on 2026-09-02.

### One mechanism detail, because the obvious reading is wrong

The regen does **not** record `bbb2` at a floor of 0. At the shrunk state its residue is below
the 8-char slack, so `--regen-shape-baseline` emits **no row at all**. The regrowth is
therefore caught as **`shape-new`** (an id absent from the baseline), not as
`shape-regrowth`. Both are ERRORs and both are distinguishable from the grandfathered standing
set, so the guarantee is unaffected -- but the mechanism is baseline ABSENCE, not a tightened
floor. The test pins this explicitly (case 5a) so that a change in the slack or the regen
emission fails loudly instead of silently swapping mechanisms.
