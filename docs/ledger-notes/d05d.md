# id:d05d

Authored directly (not relocated by `tools/ledger-shrink.py`).

## From ROADMAP

### The conflict

The ledger line grammar (`id:b048`) says an item line ENDS at its anchored id marker --
nothing after it, not even a trailing space. The archive convention appends a stub AFTER
the whole line: ` (archived -- see ROADMAP.archive.md)`.

Both are load-bearing, and they cannot both hold.

- The stub is WRITTEN by `relay/scripts/roadmap-archive.sh:114` and
  `relay/scripts/archive-closed.sh:107`.
- It is PINNED by **7 test files**.
- It accounts for roughly 55 of the 79 `grammar-item-after-id` findings in `ROADMAP.md`.

The remaining ~24 `after-id` findings are other trailing text and are a separate question;
this item is about the stub.

### Why it was refused rather than fixed

The `id:40c0` migration left all 79 in place. Moving the stub before the id marker means
editing two writers and all seven tests, which is a change to an established convention
rather than a cleanup, and `tests/` was outside that agent's write scope. Refusing and
reporting was correct.

### The options

1. **Move the stub before the id marker.** The grammar wins. Costs two writer changes and
   seven test updates, and every already-archived line stays non-conforming until rewritten
   (or is grandfathered, since archives are out of grammar scope anyway).
2. **Exempt the archive stub in the grammar.** One narrow, documented exception for a
   fixed literal. Cheap, and honest that this is a real convention -- but an exception in a
   three-shape grammar is precisely how a three-shape grammar becomes a five-shape one.
3. **Stop writing the stub.** The information (that an item was archived) may already be
   recoverable from the archive file itself, in which case the stub is redundant. Needs
   checking before it can be proposed seriously.

### Note on scope

`*.archive.md` is out of grammar scope (`todo-conformance.sh:700`, `id:2065`), so the stub
only matters on lines that carry it while still in a LIVE ledger. Worth measuring how many
of the ~55 are in that state versus already archived -- if the answer is few, option 3
becomes much more attractive and options 1 and 2 both shrink.

### Recommendation

Measure first, per the note above, then decide. I would not pick between 1 and 2 without
knowing how many live lines actually carry the stub. Option 3 is the one that dissolves the
problem rather than guarding it, which is the direction the CLAUDE.md design heuristics
prefer, and it is cheap to test.
