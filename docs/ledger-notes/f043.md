## From TODO

**A standing ruling written in a handover is invisible at the next launch, because nothing in
the relay front door reads it.** Surface the previous handover's open rulings during preflight.

## The founding incident

2026-09-11 closed with owner ruling #7 -- *"Next pool: `--once` with `relay-implementer`, then
CONSIDER making it the default"* -- and a Needs-owner item naming the same decision. The
2026-09-12 pool launched with neither `--once` nor the flag. Nothing malfunctioned: the front
door ran its full four-step preflight (`mech-preflight`, the `id:0384` currency check,
`check-install-drift`, `transcript-shape-preflight`), all green, and **none of those steps has
any notion of a handover.** The ruling was correctly written down and correctly ignored.

That is the **third** recorded non-adoption of that flag (`id:3846` records code.lawless adopting
it at 15:55 on 09-09 and dropping it by 20:18). The flag itself is now defaulted (`id:5e5a`), so
THIS instance is closed -- but the mechanism that lost it is untouched, and the next standing
ruling will be lost the same way.

## Why this is the id:4d8e shape, one level up

`id:4d8e` names the anti-pattern: a deterministic detector that FINDS a problem but whose
resolution silently no-ops. Here the "detector" is the handover itself -- a human-written record
that correctly identified the decision and its rationale -- and the resolution is a launch that
never consults it. The detection was perfect and bought nothing.

Stated as the general rule: **prose in a handover is not a mechanism.** A handover is a document
written by the previous session for the next HUMAN; treating it as a control surface for the next
RUN requires something that actually reads it at the moment of decision.

## Shape, and the trap to avoid

The cheap version is a preflight step that finds the newest `docs/session-handover-*.md` and
surfaces its `## Needs the owner` / `## OWNER RULINGS` sections in the launch notice, the same way
step 0a already surfaces the `/loop` nudge verbatim.

**The trap: do not let it become a gate.** A preflight step that BLOCKS on an unresolved ruling
would wedge every unattended `--afk` run the moment a handover carried an open question, which is
always. The `id:83c3` install-drift precedent is the right posture and was decided for exactly
this reason (owner, 2026-08-21): **warn loudly, launch anyway**, because a false positive that
silently kills an overnight run is worse than the drift it guards.

**Second trap: this must not become a parser.** A handover is free prose with varying headings;
anything that tries to EXTRACT structured rulings will rot and then quietly surface nothing -- the
silent-inert failure this item exists to prevent, reintroduced one layer down. Print the section
verbatim and let the operator (or the apex turn) read it. If a machine-readable channel is wanted
later, that is a different item: rulings would have to be WRITTEN in a structured form at close,
not mined from prose afterwards.

## Scope note

Surfacing is enough to close this. Whether the front door should then ACT on a surfaced ruling
(e.g. auto-apply a recorded flag) is deliberately NOT in scope: that turns a document written for
a human into an input that changes dispatch, which needs its own decision.
