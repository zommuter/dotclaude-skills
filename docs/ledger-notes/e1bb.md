The rule matches `\b(REMAIN|pending|activation)\b` minus an EXTERNAL-WAIT word, **with no requirement that the word sit in a gating clause at all** — so it fires on the vocabulary rather than on the structure. The seven, each checked: `1cb8` matched a **directory name** (`pending/`, a systemd PathModified target); `9ddd` matched ordinary English (*"surfaces remain singletons"*); `945e` matched a literal `stopReason` **string value** (`blocked-pending-human`); `b3a3` matched a proposed **bucket name** (`pending dispatch`); `55e1` matched an item in a list of *what a handoff doc records*; `c4b4` matched the **bug symptom being described** (*"reported it as pending human input"*). **The seventh is the nastiest shape and is worth the fix on its own: `2895`'s only match is inside the sentence that DISCHARGES the gate** — *"Blocker cleared 2026-07-01 … no longer 'pending shard-prompt work'"*. Writing the discharge **re-arms the alarm, permanently**: the more carefully an author records that a gate is cleared, the louder and more often the detector nags about it. That is an actively perverse incentive, not just noise. **Directions:** (a) require the match to sit in a gating STRUCTURE (a `gated-on:` edge, a `🚧` marker, or a clause-initial "Blocked on/until") rather than anywhere in a 1000-char line; (b) exclude negated/discharged contexts (`no longer`, `cleared`, `NOT gated`) — this is the same anchoring lesson as `id:221f`(b) and `id:259f`, a third instance of the same root; (c) if neither is cheap, **retire the class** — a detector with no true positives in 7 trains readers to ignore the whole report, which costs the classes that DO work. Note the sibling UNMARKED-GATE class is NOT in this state: its regex is tight and it produced real findings, though ~35 of its 55 hits are also FPs, mostly items whose SUBJECT is gate-parsing code and which therefore quote the vocabulary they exist to fix (`5ed7`/`b724`/`e7af` are literally the items filed to fix unanchored gate matching — and the detector trips on them).
