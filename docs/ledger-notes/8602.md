## From TODO

— observed 2026-06-18: a `/loop 30m /relay --afk …` cron re-fires `/relay` every 30 min on pure wall-clock, regardless of WHY the prior run ended. Two wasteful cases: (1) **quota stop** — relay already knows the guilty bucket's `resetTime` (`quota-stop.sh` returns per-bucket `resetTime`/`pctRemaining`, surfaced in `RELAY_STATUS.md`), but nothing feeds it back, so ticks keep firing into a capped quota until the window happens to reset; the tick should be **postponed until the guilty bucket's reset** (or be a cheap no-op that re-checks quota and exits fast). (2) **pool drain** (two empty discoveries = backlog drained) — re-discovering every 30 min until new work appears is mostly wasted; the loop should back off (longer cadence) or self-terminate until new commits/items land. The hard part is the channel: the built-in `/loop`/cron fires a fixed prompt and has no hook to read relay's exit state — so this needs either (a) the front door writing a machine-readable "next-useful-at" / "stop-reason" stamp (e.g. into `RELAY_STATUS.md` or a sidecar) that an early step of the next `/relay` run reads to decide "exit immediately, nothing to do until T", or (b) a relay-aware loop wrapper that schedules its own next wake from the stop reason (cf. ScheduleWakeup dynamic mode) instead of a dumb fixed cron. Shares the "no reliable in-`/loop` marker" root cause with id:888a (loop-hint can't tell a manual `--afk` from a loop tick — same missing channel). Needs a small design note before building; low-cost-but-non-obvious. <!-- id:8602 -->
